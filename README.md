# A Walkable History of Art

**[Visit the museum](https://artmuseum.artfrompixels.com)** · [Watch the one-minute tour](demo/museum-demo.mp4)

A museum you can walk through in your browser. Travel along a timeline, choose
an artist, and step into a gallery of their work.

The aim is to make art history easier to explore: see who worked at the same
time, follow an artist's work through the years, and spend time with paintings
beyond the handful everyone recognises. Wikipedia's knowledge becomes a place
you can visit, freely, without an account.

## What you can do

- **Explore more than a thousand years of painting.** Browse 35 periods and
  traditions, including Chinese, Japanese, Indian, Persian and European art.
- **Find your own starting point.** Begin with Featured painters or choose
  All artists for the full collection of **572 artists and about 100,000
  works**. Switch between the Gallery Wall and Star Map, zoom into a period, or
  search for a name in Explore (it forgives typos), by continent or country.
- **Walk through an artist's work.** Galleries bring together the works
  available from the sources, with no limit to famous paintings. Rooms follow
  the years, or the phases of the artist's life where they are known
  (Picasso's Blue and Rose periods ...), with room styles and music chosen for
  each period. At the end, doors lead to the artist before and the artist after.
- **Make a room.** Hang works by many artists side by side, by era, movement,
  school, genre, country or museum; search for artists and paintings to add,
  drag works into your order and onto other floors, drag the floors up and
  down, and share the link. Or walk the Louvre, the Rijksmuseum and the
  National Gallery of Greece as recreated rooms.
- **Look closer.** Approach a painting, click it to inspect it, and read about
  the work: its era, movement, school and genre, the works before and after it,
  what others painted the same year, or the original image on its own. Wall
  labels show recorded dimensions when available and whether the display is at
  original scale, reduced or enlarged.
- **Watch the films.** A gallery whose artist has films (documentaries,
  television portraits, archive footage, linked from their Wikipedia articles
  in every language) has a screening room beside its entrance room, in the
  gallery's own style: walk through the curtain (or the top bar's **Films**),
  sit among the chairs, and the projector throws the film on the screen while
  the lights dim.

On a computer, use **W A S D** to move (at a run; the settings have walking
speeds) and the mouse to look, **Space** to jump, **C** to sit, **G** for the
audio guide and **O** for the settings: speed, the canvas texture, the audio
guide's voice (the browser's, or a natural AI voice run on your computer, a
one-time 330 MB download you choose) and
reading speed, and hiding the controls (**H**).
On a phone, drag to look and tap the floor to walk. Click or tap a painting to
inspect it. **Explore → How to explore** brings back the short guide on the
timeline.

The collection is a starting point, and coverage is uneven. Featured is an
editorial introduction to influential painters, with its
[selection explained here](docs/FEATURED_ARTISTS.md). The museum will keep
growing as more artists and works become available in the sources.

## Made possible by Wikipedia

Artist biographies, artwork information and images come from
**[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/), [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/)
and [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/)**. The museum brings
that material together; artist selection and timeline groupings are editorial
choices. Source links and image credits let you explore further.

Recorded sizes help convey scale, but very large or small works are adjusted
for the gallery. Some dates, dimensions and images are unavailable. This is an
educational way to explore the collection, with room for corrections and
better coverage.

The volunteers who write, photograph, catalogue and maintain this knowledge
make the museum possible. If you enjoy it, please
**[donate to Wikipedia](https://donate.wikimedia.org/)**. Free knowledge keeps
democracies strong.
